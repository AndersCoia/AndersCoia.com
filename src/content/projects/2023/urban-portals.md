## Overview
I’ve had an interest in urban and domestic spaces for quite a while. Living in them my entire life, I’ve learnt to appreciate their quaint and repetitive forms. The rental I currently live in is falling apart and will likely be demolished in the foreseeable future, yet I still feel attachment to its lonely, broken-down silhouette in a developed street.

**Urban Portals** explores that tension—how places we overlook can still hold beauty, presence, and a strange kind of meaning.

## Concept
I found that garages—often the most overlooked room of the house—provided the perfect “doors” to another world. Their square shape and loud presence in the urban environment creates a window with just enough room to suggest what could lie beyond.

I find it quite amusing: garages are mundane and rarely perceived beyond the amount of room they have to store things. This work leans into that contradiction, using the garage door as a frame to defy preconceptions about what’s behind it.

## Intent
My intention is to have the viewer reconsider the way they perceive urban spaces—using an unexpected, slightly uncanny effect to show that some things are not as boring as they seem.

## Approach
- **Subject selection:** Focused on repeated architectural forms that are easy to recognise and easy to ignore.
- **Framing:** Composed each image as a “window” to create a sense of threshold.
- **Tone:** Balanced familiarity and subtle surrealism to keep the work suggestive rather than literal.

## Role
Concept, photography, sequencing.
