## Overview
The goal of this visualisation is to show that Australia experiences significantly more earthquakes than many think—while giving the viewer a clear reason why this misconception exists.

Starting from the lens of the earthquake felt by Canberra residents in September 2021, the story expands outward to show that earthquakes aren’t rare—just the ones we feel.

## Core Message
- Earthquakes occur often, but most are too small to notice.
- As magnitude increases, power increases dramatically—small changes have big impact.

## Process
### Technical ideation
Instead of beginning with hand drawing as usual, I started in Excel and used its charting tools to quickly test how the data could be formatted in an understandable and accessible way. This proved efficient: it exposed weaknesses in my initial assumptions about how to represent magnitude and power.

Originally, I wanted to plot magnitude against power directly, but it was confusing and needed too much explaining.

### Narrative structure
I chose to keep time on the x-axis, and shift the y-axis from magnitude to relative power during the visualisation to show the difference between “what we feel” and “what’s actually happening”.

To support clarity and balance, I extended the dataset beyond the initial period so the chart had enough structure to read as a story rather than an isolated spike.

## Design Decisions
- Earthy palette to represent the ground, with stronger graph colours for legibility.
- The September event highlighted in red as the anchor point.
- Reduced axis detail so the focus stays on the relationship between charts rather than exact values.
- An opening motion concept to suggest seismic activity before the data story begins (with the awareness that motion alone can read as a general “shake” rather than a specific symbol).

## Outcome
A clear concept direction and a set of sketches/prototypes that communicate frequency vs perception, and magnitude vs power—without requiring heavy explanation.

## Role
Information design, data sketching, prototyping, motion concepting.
