## Context
When it comes to displaying prestige, the ability to nurture students, and the extent of facilities, a graduate show is one of the most important events of the year. There are two key audiences: people with a direct relationship to graduates, and people with an interest in the institution—partners, sponsors, and potential new students.

The website needs to demonstrate value with confidence, skill, and talent—while letting student work lead the experience.

## Goals
- Present the exhibition with a sense of confidence and craft.
- Make it easy to move from the homepage to a student profile.
- Support both purposeful navigation and exploratory browsing.
- Maintain a distinctive identity without distracting from work.

## Research Highlights
### Reference 1: Grad show websites that feel clean and confident
A strong benchmark for consistency and composition: clear hierarchy, student work placed at the forefront, and restrained calls-to-action that keep browsing smooth.

A key learning here was how a persistent navigation bar can act as an anchor and provide subtle affordances (like underlining) that help users understand where they are—without dominating the page.

### Reference 2: Gallery-style conventions (and where they break)
The “digital white” gallery approach can be effective when it supports the work, but it can also become bloated on large screens if hierarchy and composition aren’t carefully controlled. Interestingly, the same approach can feel far better on mobile, where grid and scrolling naturally support clarity.

### Reference 3: Breaking convention with colour and interaction
A more playful benchmark stood out through off-white tones, organic shapes, and strong hover states that reveal artwork/artist details in a way that encourages exploration.

## Process
### Step back: clarify the journey
Before wireframes, I mapped the user journey—especially the path from homepage to a student profile. This was the key function: some users will browse, but many will actively search for specific people.

I wanted a one-page homepage because it’s an efficient way to communicate navigation, conventions, and style without overwhelming users.

### Mood, atmosphere, and motif
I explored the atmosphere of the physical environment and translated it into digital cues—focusing on “mundane” details that define the experience: table colour, wall indents, floor patterns.

From that, I developed a concise palette and a simple linear motif inspired by repeated visual lines that guide people through the space. I also intentionally explored a non-white background option to support:
- Night-time accessibility
- A more distinct identity

## Deliverables
- User journey mapping + structural planning
- Moodboard + palette and motif exploration
- Desktop and mobile wireframes
- Social post concepts for promotion

## Role
UX research, information architecture, UI concepting, wireframing.
